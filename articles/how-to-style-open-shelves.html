How to Style Open Shelves Without Clutter

Open shelves look effortless when they follow a system and chaotic when they follow nothing. The system is simple.

By the Room by Room Editorial Team · 2026

In this guide

  1. Use the rule of thirds per shelf
  2. Limit yourself to one accent colour
  3. Stack, lean, and layer for depth
  4. In a kitchen, keep it functional

Use the rule of thirds per shelf

Divide each shelf into thirds: one third objects, one third books or verticals, one third empty. The empty third is the part people skip, and it is what makes shelves look calm rather than crowded.

Fill from one side of the shelf, not from the middle. Asymmetry reads as considered; symmetry with gaps reads as unfinished.

Limit yourself to one accent colour

Choose one colour that appears three times across the whole unit, and keep everything else neutral. Three repeats look intentional; two look like an accident; five look like a shop display.

Let the material carry the variety instead: ceramic, glass, wood, linen. The same restraint that guides mixing wood tones applies here.

Stack, lean, and layer for depth

  • Stack flat items: bowls, boards, folded textiles.
  • Lean artwork or large plates against the back.
  • Layer by putting something taller behind and lower in front.

Depth is what stops shelves reading as flat wallpaper. Even 5 cm of overlap is enough.

In a kitchen, keep it functional

Kitchen shelves hold grease and steam, so whatever is on them must be washable and used often. Skip paper, unsealed wood, and anything you would mind cleaning monthly.

Keep the daily items within reach and the rest in cupboards — the zoning approach in kitchen storage is the better answer for volume.

Small Entryway Solutions That Hold Up Daily

An entryway fails on the days you arrive with bags. Design it for that moment, not for the tidy version.

By the Room by Room Editorial Team · 2026

In this guide

  1. Give every arrival a landing spot
  2. Protect 90 cm of walkway
  3. Cap the shoe storage deliberately
  4. Choose floor and paint for traffic

Give every arrival a landing spot

You need somewhere to put keys, post, and a bag within one step of the door. A small shelf or a shallow console does it; the top of a shoe cabinet does not, because it fills with shoes.

Add a hook at adult shoulder height for the bag you use daily, and a second row lower down for children.

Protect 90 cm of walkway

Hallways are circulation, not storage. Keep 90 cm clear where two people pass, and never let a bench or cabinet narrow the route to the stairs.

If the space is narrower than 1.2 m, use depth instead of width: wall-mounted storage, shallow shelves, and hooks. The walkway logic matches living room layout.

Cap the shoe storage deliberately

Decide how many pairs live by the door — usually four per person in summer, six in winter — and buy a cabinet for exactly that. Anything more migrates to bedrooms.

Tilted shoe racks or pull-out drawers use depth better than flat shelves in a narrow hall. Keep a tray for wet boots, and wipe it weekly rather than annually.

Choose floor and paint for traffic

The entry takes more abrasion than any other room. A washable mid-sheen paint and a washable runner will look better after two years than flat paint and pale carpet.

Dark floors hide scuffs; light floors show grit. A medium tone with a matte finish is the most forgiving. Paint longevity is covered in choosing paint colours that last.

Editor’s note

Measurements, product availability, and prices change. Check current details before ordering, and measure your own space rather than trusting a standard size.
